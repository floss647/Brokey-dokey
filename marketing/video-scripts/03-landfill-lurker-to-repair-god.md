# BROKEYDOBKEY — "LANDFILL LURKER TO REPAIR GOD"
## 4-Part YouTube Series Bible
### Documentary Format | 8–12 Minutes Per Episode | Comedy/Factual

---

## SERIES OVERVIEW

**Format:** BBC Three-style documentary. Deadpan narration. Real people. Real broken stuff. Real money made. Structured like a nature documentary, but the animals are broken Xboxes and the ecosystem is BrokeyDokey.

**Tone:** David Attenborough narrating a car boot sale. Think *Taskmaster* energy meets *The Repair Shop* — if The Repair Shop had less wholesomeness and more financial incentive.

**The Arc:** Each episode follows one hero character through the BrokeyDokey journey — from "I have a pile of broken electronics I've been ignoring since 2019" to "I actually made real money this month." They move up one or more gamification ranks per episode.

**Series Arc:** Four different heroes. Four different starting points. Same destination: they made money from junk, they helped someone else, and they didn't fill a landfill.

**Filming style:** Handheld, naturalistic. No studio lighting. No autocue. Let people be awkward — that's funnier.

**Narrator:** Dry, RP British accent. Imagine a nature documentary narrator who's been told he must now cover broken electronics. He's coping.

## EPISODE 1: "THE HOARDER AWAKENS"

**Runtime:** 10–12 minutes  
**Gamification rank journey:** Landfill Lurker → Scrap Apprentice  
**Upload timing:** Early January (New Year clear-out energy is peak)

---

### HERO CHARACTER

**Name:** Dave  
**Age:** 47  
**Occupation:** Retired IT admin  
**Location:** Sheffield  
**Personality:** Quietly stubborn, softly funny, deeply in denial about how much broken tech he owns  
**Starting rank:** Landfill Lurker (he doesn't know this yet)  
**Broken gear pile:** 3 dead laptops, 2 broken printers, a PS3 with disc read errors, an original Xbox, a broken tablet, and a wireless router that he's kept "in case."

---

### EPISODE DESCRIPTION

Dave has a spare room he refers to as "the office." It is not an office. It is a monument to procrastination, populated entirely by broken electronics he has been "meaning to sort out" since approximately 2017. His wife has given him one final warning. This is that warning being acted upon. Probably.

---

### SCENES

**SCENE 1 — THE REVEAL (0:00 – 1:30)**

> [VISUAL] Dave opens the door to the spare room. Camera pans slowly. It is worse than anticipated.

> [NARRATOR] *"The male of the species has evolved a remarkable defence mechanism when confronted with broken technology: he puts it in a room, closes the door, and never thinks about it again. Sheffield's Dave has been running this system for nine years."*

> [VISUAL] Dave picks up the original Xbox.

> [DAVE] "That's still good, that. Just needs... something."

> [NARRATOR] *"It does not still need something. It needs last rites."*

---

**SCENE 2 — THE AUDIT (1:30 – 3:00)**

> [VISUAL] Dave sits on the floor of the room, surrounded by items. He's made tea. This will take a while.

> [VISUAL — INSERT] He opens BrokeyDokey, starts scanning items one by one. The AI valuations appear on screen.

> | Item | Dave's Estimate | BrokeyDokey Valuation |
> |------|----------------|----------------------|
> | Original Xbox | "Probably worthless" | £45 for parts |
> | PS3 with disc errors | "Might fix itself" | £70 repaired |
> | Broken Dell laptop | "Useless" | £55 repaired |
> | Second broken laptop | "Also useless" | £40 repaired |
> | Broken tablet | "Landfill" | £25 repaired |

> [VISUAL] Dave stares at his phone. Then at the room.

> [DAVE] "...Right."

> [NARRATOR] *"Dave has, conservatively, two hundred and thirty-five pounds sitting in a room he calls 'the office.' The irony is not lost on him. Actually, it is completely lost on him."*

---

**SCENE 3 — THE LISTING MONTAGE (3:00 – 5:00)**

> [VISUAL] Dave learns to use BrokeyDokey. It is a journey.

> [VISUAL] First attempt: He photographs the PS3. His thumb is in every shot.

> [VISUAL] Second attempt: Better. The AI damage assessment reads the photos and flags the disc drive error.

> [VISUAL] Dave reads the description the AI has auto-drafted: *"PS3 with disc read error, cosmetic wear, powers on, untested for full functionality."*

> [DAVE] "That's exactly what it is. How did it know that?"

> [NARRATOR] *"Dave has discovered machine learning. He is the last person in Britain to do so."*

> [VISUAL] He lists five items. Sets prices. Activates BrokeyGuard™ escrow on each one.

> [DAVE] "So nobody can do me over?"

> [VISUAL] He reads the BrokeyGuard™ explanation on screen.

> [DAVE] "Right. I like that."

---

**SCENE 4 — THE WAIT (5:00 – 6:30)**

> [VISUAL] Dave is watching TV. His phone buzzes. He ignores it. It buzzes again. He picks it up.

> [VISUAL — PHONE SCREEN] "First offer received: £22 for the PS3."

> [DAVE] *[to his wife, off-camera]* "Kath! Someone wants to buy the PS3!"

> [VOICE OFF-CAMERA — KATH] "I don't know what that means."

> [DAVE] "It means I'm a businessman."

> [NARRATOR] *"Dave is not yet a businessman. He is a Scrap Apprentice. But for now, we let him have this."*

---

**SCENE 5 — THE FIRST SALE (6:30 – 8:30)**

> [VISUAL] Dave packs the PS3 into a padded envelope. He has used too much bubble wrap. An entire roll of bubble wrap. The parcel is spherical.

> [NARRATOR] *"The BrokeyDokey platform facilitates secure peer-to-peer sales of broken tech. What it cannot facilitate is Dave's relationship with bubble wrap."*

> [VISUAL] He drops the parcel at the post office. Looks satisfied.

> [VISUAL — 3 days later] Payment confirmation hits his phone. £22. He screenshots it.

> [DAVE] "That's £22 I didn't have yesterday."

> [NARRATOR] *"Dave has cleared one item from the spare room. Fourteen remain. He has, however, upgraded his status on BrokeyDokey from Landfill Lurker to Scrap Apprentice. He doesn't know what that means yet. He will."*

---

**SCENE 6 — THE CLOSE (8:30 – 10:00)**

> [VISUAL] Dave stands in the spare room. One shelf is now empty. He looks at it.

> [DAVE] "I could put a proper desk in here."

> [NARRATOR] *"Dave will not put a proper desk in there. But he might list three more items this weekend. And that, in its own way, is progress."*

> [ON-SCREEN GRAPHIC — End card]
> **DAVE'S MONTH 1 TOTAL: £22**
> **RANK: SCRAP APPRENTICE**
> **ITEMS DIVERTED FROM LANDFILL: 1**

## EPISODE 2: "THE GAMER WHO BROKE TOO MUCH"

**Runtime:** 10 minutes  
**Gamification rank journey:** Scrap Apprentice → Component Collector  
**Upload timing:** February (post-Christmas gaming binge, controller carnage season)

---

### HERO CHARACTER

**Name:** Priya  
**Age:** 24  
**Occupation:** Graphic designer, freelance  
**Location:** Manchester  
**Personality:** Chaotic, funny, entirely self-aware about her gaming rage, treats this like a side hustle project  
**Starting rank:** Scrap Apprentice (she already listed one thing last month)  
**Broken gear pile:** 4 controllers (assorted causes), a cracked Switch screen, a broken gaming headset, a dead PS5 disc drive, a retro SNES with no video output

---

### EPISODE DESCRIPTION

Priya breaks controllers. Not because she has a temper — or rather, not *only* because she has a temper — but because she plays a lot and buys cheap ones. Her flat has a designated drawer for broken controllers. She calls it "the drawer of shame." BrokeyDokey's AI calls it "a revenue stream."

---

### SCENES

**SCENE 1 — THE DRAWER OF SHAME (0:00 – 1:30)**

> [VISUAL] Priya opens a kitchen drawer. It is entirely filled with broken controllers. She opens it with the energy of someone revealing a crime scene they are both guilty of and proud of.

> [PRIYA] "I know."

> [NARRATOR] *"Priya knows."*

---

**SCENE 2 — THE AUDIT & THE ROASTING (1:30 – 3:30)**

> [VISUAL] She uses BrokeyDokey's "Roast My Junk" feature on each controller. She reads the AI roasts aloud.

> [PRIYA] *[reading]* "This controller has clearly been used as a stress ball, a frisbee, and possibly a defensive weapon." *[to camera]* Yeah, fair.

> [NARRATOR] *"The AI identifies £165 in sellable components across the drawer's contents. Priya had previously described the drawer as 'a write-off.' The AI disagrees."*

---

**SCENE 3 — THE SIDE HUSTLE PLAN (3:30 – 5:00)**

> [VISUAL] Priya makes a spreadsheet. She is a designer — it is colour-coded, beautiful, and excessive.

> [PRIYA] "Right. So if I list all of these, and they all sell, and they all get repaired and resold, I've basically made —" *[taps calculator]* "— nearly two hundred quid."

> [NARRATOR] *"She has. She also buys about two hundred pounds of controllers per year. We will let her enjoy this moment before the maths catches up with her."*

---

**SCENE 4 — THE RETRO DISCOVERY (5:00 – 7:00)**

> [VISUAL] Priya finds the SNES while listing. She doesn't remember owning it.

> [PRIYA] "Where did this come from?"

> [VISUAL] She photographs it on BrokeyDokey. The AI valuation: £95 repaired.

> [PRIYA] *[genuinely startled]* "Ninety-five pounds?!"

> [NARRATOR] *"The retro gaming market is a seller's paradise for people who inherited, forgot, or accidentally acquired classic hardware. Priya has accidentally acquired a SNES. She now intends to acquire money instead."*

---

**SCENE 5 — SALES MONTAGE (7:00 – 9:00)**

> [VISUAL] Fast-cut: listings going up, buyers appearing, BrokeyGuard™ escrow confirming, parcels going out. Priya's BrokeyDokey rank ticks upward. Component Collector.

> [VISUAL] She reads the rank description on-screen.

> [PRIYA] *[reading]* "Component Collector: You know that broken gear has parts worth more than the bin bag you were going to put it in." *[pause]* That's actually really rude.

---

**SCENE 6 — THE CLOSE (9:00 – 10:00)**

> [VISUAL] Priya looks at the empty drawer of shame. It is now just a kitchen drawer.

> [PRIYA] "I mean, I'm probably going to fill it again."

> [NARRATOR] *"She will. But she now knows what to do about it."*

> [ON-SCREEN GRAPHIC]
> **PRIYA'S MONTH 2 TOTAL: £163**
> **RANK: COMPONENT COLLECTOR**
> **ITEMS DIVERTED FROM LANDFILL: 8**

## EPISODE 3: "THE ACCIDENTAL ENTREPRENEUR"

**Runtime:** 11 minutes  
**Gamification rank journey:** Component Collector → Soldering Sage  
**Upload timing:** April (spring clean season, people finding old tech)

---

### HERO CHARACTER

**Name:** Marcus  
**Age:** 31  
**Occupation:** Van driver, part-time  
**Location:** Birmingham  
**Personality:** Underestimates himself constantly. Is actually very good at fixing things. Doesn't know this yet.  
**Starting rank:** Component Collector  
**Broken gear pile:** Old laptops from his mum's house, a broken drone he found at a car boot sale for £8, two dead phones, a sack full of PC components from a mate who "upgraded"

---

### EPISODE DESCRIPTION

Marcus doesn't think of himself as someone who fixes things. He thinks of himself as someone who drives a van. But when his drone arrives and the AI repair guide walks him through the fix step by step, something clicks. Not just the drone arm. Something in Marcus.

---

### SCENES

**SCENE 1 — THE BOOT SALE DRONE (0:00 – 2:00)**

> [VISUAL] Marcus at a car boot sale, holding a smashed drone he's just bought for £8 from a man in a hi-vis jacket.

> [MARCUS] "The man said it just needs a propeller. I think it might need more than a propeller."

> [NARRATOR] *"It needs more than a propeller. It needs two new arms, a replacement camera mount, and a firmware reset. Marcus has paid eight pounds for this. He is, depending on your perspective, either a fool or an investor."*

---

**SCENE 2 — THE VALUATION SHOCK (2:00 – 3:30)**

> [VISUAL] Marcus opens BrokeyDokey on his phone, photographs the drone. The valuation appears.

> [VISUAL — PHONE SCREEN] "Estimated repaired value: £145"

> [MARCUS] *[staring]* "From eight quid?"

> [NARRATOR] *"From eight quid. Marcus may be an investor."*

---

**SCENE 3 — THE AI REPAIR GUIDE (3:30 – 6:00)**

> [VISUAL] Marcus opens the BrokeyDokey AI repair guide for his drone model. It walks him through the fix with animated diagrams, step-by-step instructions, and part sourcing links.

> [MARCUS] *[following along]* "Right. So that bit goes there... and I need a T6 screwdriver..."

> [NARRATOR] *"Marcus has never repaired a drone. He has, however, repaired a transit van engine, rewired a kitchen, and once fixed a dishwasher using only a YouTube video and stubbornness. He is more qualified than he thinks."*

> [MARCUS] *[holding the repaired drone]* "That's... actually all right, that."

**SCENE 4 — THE TEST FLIGHT (6:00 – 7:00)**

> [VISUAL] Marcus takes the drone to a park. He's nervous. He launches it. It flies. A clean, stable hover.

> [MARCUS] "Oh."

**SCENE 5 — THE LISTING AND THE ECO-CREDITS (7:00 – 9:00)**

> [NARRATOR] *"Marcus gets money AND points. He finds this, proportionally, even more satisfying than the money."*

> [VISUAL] His rank ticks to Soldering Sage. He reads the description.

> [MARCUS] *[quietly]* "Soldering Sage." *[beat]* Yeah. I'll take that.

**SCENE 6 — THE CLOSE (9:00 – 11:00)**

> [VISUAL] Marcus looks at the remaining pile from his mum's house. He picks up a dead laptop. Opens BrokeyDokey. Photographs it. Reads the repair guide. Nods.

> [NARRATOR] *"Marcus has discovered that he is, in addition to being a van driver, an electronics repair technician, an entrepreneur, and a Soldering Sage. He hasn't told his mum yet. She'll be chuffed."*

> [ON-SCREEN GRAPHIC]
> **MARCUS'S MONTH 3 TOTAL: £287**
> **RANK: SOLDERING SAGE**
> **ITEMS DIVERTED FROM LANDFILL: 6**
> **ECO-CREDITS EARNED: 340**

## EPISODE 4: "THE REPAIR GOD COMETH"

**Runtime:** 12 minutes  
**Gamification rank journey:** Soldering Sage → Repair God  
**Upload timing:** June (summer clean-out, school year ending, students dumping stuff)

---

### HERO CHARACTER

**Name:** Bex  
**Age:** 38  
**Occupation:** Former retail manager, now full-time BrokeyDokey seller  
**Location:** Bristol  
**Personality:** Brutally efficient, drily funny, takes absolutely zero nonsense, but secretly delighted by all of this  
**Starting rank:** Soldering Sage (she's been on BrokeyDokey six months)  
**This episode:** Her first month crossing £500. The Repair God rank unlocks. She's insufferable about it.

---

### EPISODE DESCRIPTION

Bex left a retail management job that was, in her words, "soul-leaking." She now spends her mornings buying broken tech from Facebook Marketplace and charity shops, repairing it using BrokeyDokey's AI guides, and reselling it at profit. She is very good at this. She is aware she is very good at this. This episode is about the moment she becomes officially, algorithmically, a Repair God.

---

> [NARRATOR] *"Bex runs a one-woman operation from a converted garage in Bristol. She sources broken tech, repairs it with the help of BrokeyDokey's AI repair guides, and resells it. ---

**SCENE 2 — THE SOURCING RUN (2:00 – 4:00)**

> [BEX] *[to the phone, not to anyone]* "You're coming with me."

## SERIES PRODUCTION NOTES

**Episode order:** Drop weekly. January, February, April, June aligns with peak clear-out seasons.

**Narrator casting:** Real casting essential. Tone must be genuinely deadpan — not winking, not trying to be funny. The comedy comes from the contrast between the narrator's gravity and the subject matter.

**End card consistency:** Every episode ends with the same three stats: money made, rank achieved, items diverted from landfill. This is the emotional payoff and the metric that earns the brand message.

**Cross-episode continuity:** Small callbacks. Dave buys something Priya listed. Marcus's repaired drone ends up on Bex's sourcing run. Keep the ecosystem feeling connected.

**Series title card design:** Brutal yellow-on-black. "LANDFILL LURKER TO REPAIR GOD" in the BrokeyDokey brand font. Each episode subtitle below in white.
