# BROKEYDOBKEY — BRAND LAUNCH VIDEO
## "Don't Bin It. Bank It."
### 60-Second Hero Film

---

## OVERVIEW

**Duration:** 60 seconds  
**Format:** Cinematic 16:9, 4K  
**Tone:** Deadpan British comedy meets slick product film. Think Peaky Blinders pacing with a Money Supermarket budget and a Partridge sensibility.  
**Music:** Starts tense/dramatic (orchestral stabs), transitions to upbeat indie-electronic at the 30-second mark.

## FULL SCRIPT

---

**[0:00 – 0:05] — THE CRIME**

> [VISUAL] Extreme close-up: a thumb mashing a controller button with increasingly psychotic pressure. The plastic creaks.

> [SOUND DESIGN] No music. Just the click-click-click of a button being abused. A TV crowd cheering distantly.

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] — none —

---

**[0:05 – 0:10] — THE MOMENT**

> [VISUAL] Slow-motion. The controller releases from a hand. It spins, lazily, with the grace of a man who has given up entirely. It hits the wall.

> [SOUND DESIGN] Orchestral hit. Single dramatic bass note. The crack of plastic. Silence.

> [VOICEOVER — dry, matter-of-fact, RP British accent with a hint of tired compassion]
> *"Every year, British people throw away 23 million pieces of tech."*

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] **23,000,000 PIECES.**

---

**[0:10 – 0:18] — THE RECKONING**

> [VISUAL] Our man, mid-30s, sits on his sofa and stares at the broken controller on the floor. The TV is still showing his defeat screen. He doesn't move. The dog watches him from across the room with quiet judgement.

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"Some of it from natural causes."*

> [VISUAL CUT] Close-up of the controller, one stick pointing hopelessly sideways.

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"Some of it from a momentary lapse in emotional regulation."*

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] **"A MOMENTARY LAPSE IN EMOTIONAL REGULATION"**

---

**[0:18 – 0:25] — THE DISCOVERY**

> [VISUAL] He picks up his phone. Scrolls past "bin collection day" reminders on Google. Finds BrokeyDokey. Opens it. The UI is clean, fast, confident.

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"Either way. It doesn't have to end in a bin bag."*

> [VISUAL] The app's camera guides him to photograph the controller. He does it. Broken bits and all. A valuation appears:*"Estimated repaired value: £35. List it now."*

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] **"£35. LIST IT NOW."**

> [SOUND DESIGN] A satisfying ping. The music shifts — upbeat, purposeful, slightly cocky.

---

**[0:25 – 0:40] — THE ECOSYSTEM**

> [VISUAL — FAST MONTAGE, cuts every 1-2 seconds]

> Shot: He lists it. Three taps. "LISTED."  
> Shot: Notification — "Someone's interested."  
> Shot: BrokeyGuard™ escrow badge appears on screen. Text: "Your money's protected. Obvs."  
> Shot: Padded envelope. Label printed. Dropped at post box.  
> Shot: A repair workshop. Spartan. Functional. Very north of England.  
> Shot: Young woman, headphones on, opens the parcel. Examines the controller with the calm of someone who has seen worse.  
> Shot: AI repair guide on tablet — animated, step-by-step, almost cheerful.  
> Shot: Soldering. Snapping. Clicking. Time-lapse.  
> Shot: Repaired controller held up. It looks *brand new.*

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"BrokeyDokey connects the people who broke it with the people who fix it. And the people who want to buy it."*

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT — flashing in rhythm] **BROKE IT. FIX IT. BANK IT.**

---

**[0:40 – 0:52] — THE PAYOFF**

> [VISUAL] Back to our man. His phone buzzes. He looks. Payment received. He looks at the wall where the controller hit. Small dent. He shrugs.

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"The broken stuff in your house is worth more than you think."*

> [VISUAL] Quick-cut montage: cracked phone, dead laptop, snapped drone arm, a graphics card so dusty it looks geological — each one flashing a BrokeyDokey valuation over it. £45. £120. £80. £200.

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] **YOUR JUNK. THEIR GOLD.**

> [VOICEOVER]
> *"Someone out there wants it. Someone else can fix it. All you have to do is list it."*

---

**[0:52 – 1:00] — THE SIGN-OFF**

> [VISUAL] Black screen. Yellow text slams in one word at a time.

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT]
> **DON'T**
> **BIN IT.**
> **BANK IT.**

> [VISUAL] BrokeyDokey logo appears. Clean. Bold. Yellow on black.

> [VOICEOVER — same deadpan, one beat, perfectly timed]
> *"BrokeyDokey. Obviously."*

> [ON-SCREEN TEXT] **BROKEYDOBKEY.CO.UK** | Available on App Store + Google Play

> [SOUND DESIGN] Single note. Silence. Done.

## POST-PRODUCTION NOTES

**Colour grade:** High contrast. Near black-and-white in the opening reckoning scenes. Colour saturates in — gradually warm and golden — as the controller enters the BrokeyDokey ecosystem. By the montage, full warm colour. This is the visual metaphor.

**Typography:** All on-screen text in BrokeyDokey brand font. Bold. Uppercase. Yellow or white on black. No fancy transitions — just hard cuts, as if slapped on screen.

**Sound design is critical:** The silence in the reckoning scene must hold. The ping of the valuation notification should feel genuinely satisfying — like a slot machine hit. The tonal shift in music at 0:25 is the emotional turn of the whole film.

**Subtitles:** Full subtitles on the voiceover for all versions. Also create a no-audio/subtitles-only cut for social.

**Cut-down versions needed:**
- 30-second cut (cut from 0:10 to 0:25, shorten montage)
- 15-second cut (reckoning → valuation → sign-off only)
- 6-second bumper (slo-mo controller throw → "Don't Bin It. Bank It.")

**Aspect ratio exports:** 16:9 (YouTube/TV), 1:1 (Instagram/Facebook), 9:16 (Reels/TikTok/Shorts) — reframe accordingly, montage works best vertically.
